01

Vision and principles

ReadMe.txt

bytebent makes small, odd effects for producers who like happy accidents. Each plugin borrows a mechanism from somewhere outside music (data compression, error-correcting codes, synchronising pendulum clocks) or models how a body actually makes sound (the human larynx and mouth, a dolphin's phonic lips, a bird's syrinx), then turns it into a sound you can perform and pushes it past what any real animal can do. Strange on purpose, built to be played.

Product principles

  • One idea per plugin. Each product has a single concept you can explain in one line ("only N kB can change per second").
  • One knob that matters. The core concept lives on one hero control (Budget, Coupling, Bit depth). Everything else shapes it.
  • Show the invisible. Every plugin has a live view of its hidden state: fresh vs. stale audio, territory, rank lines.
  • Sidechain powered. Most products take a second input so one track can reshape another.
  • Names say what they do. Product names are "what it works on + what it does to it" (Bandwidth Brawl). No abstract or alliterative names.

Quality principles

  • Never crash the host. Real-time safe DSP: nothing allocated, locked or loaded on the audio thread.
  • Same sound at any buffer size. Fixed internal control blocks, latency reported to the DAW.
  • Repeatable renders. Seed plus reset-on-play, so a bounce sounds the same every time.
  • Reference first. A Python or JavaScript reference defines the sound; the C++ port is tested against it.
  • Honest about how it's made. Decided Never claim the plugins are made without AI. AI help can be mentioned plainly, e.g. "designed by me, built with AI help." The showcase's old "NO AI PRESETS" badge was removed for this reason.
  • Science-based bodies. The animal instruments start from published research, kept in each repo's research.md, and stay inside human hearing range.

The six stripes are the brand mark: the full stack belongs to the company and to Bandwidth Crusher. Other products usually lead with one stripe (or a pair) as their identity.

One accent per product is a guideline, not a hard rule. A single colour keeps a product easy to recognise, and a pair suits a two-input "versus" product (Brawl uses blue for A and red for B). Voice

  • Plain, short, a little dry. Say what the plugin does to your audio in one line: "Two tracks. One loop. One budget. Let them fight."
  • Explain the mechanism, then the sound: "Whatever doesn't get updated keeps playing old audio: smears, ghosts, stutters and fizz."
  • No hype words ("revolutionary", "game-changing"). Let the demo audio do the selling.
  • Era jokes are welcome in small doses: 88×31 badges, a visitor counter, "best viewed at 800×600".

04

Product line

Catalogue

Four families: the Bandwidth Series, the Voice pair, the Creatures and the Lab. Names shown are the current working names; renames still under discussion are listed with each card and in Open questions.

The Bandwidth Series

The flagship family, inspired by "Bad Apple but only N kB can change per second" videos. A short loop plays on repeat; the input tries to rewrite it under a strict byte budget.

Bandwidth Crusher

v0.1.1 shipped

Only N kB of the loop may change per second. Whatever isn't updated keeps playing stale audio: smears, ghosts, stutter and fizz.

Controls: Budget 0.5–1000 kB/s, Bit depth 2–16, Greedy ↔ Random, Error threshold, Loop 1 ms–4 s or 1/64–4 bars synced, Memory, Chunk 1–4096 smp, Edge smoothing, In/Out gain, Dry/Wet, Shared/Split stereo budget, Reset on play, Seed.

Formats: AU, VST3, Standalone · macOS universal · 10 ms reported latency · tested in Ableton Live

Rename? "Bandwidth Crush" pairs better with Brawl Open

Bandwidth Brawl

v0.1.0 built

Your track (A) and a sidechain (B) share one loop and one budget. A side only claims budget where its own input has changed since it last wrote there; the loser freezes on stale audio. Ducking that stalls instead of dips.

Fight: Budget, Bias A↔B, Sharpness, Hold, Savings · Buffer: Loop, Chunk, Smoothing, Memory, Bits, Random · I/O: B Trim, Mix, Output

Views: Territory strip (blue = A, red = B, grey = unclaimed) and a tug-of-war budget meter

Formats: AU, VST3, Standalone · macOS universal · main input + sidechain bus · 10 ms reported latency · plugin code Bwbr

State: Full plugin in the repo: C++ DSP, JUCE wrapper, web UI, Python reference, compare tests, browser bench and Mac CI. Name Decided

The Creatures

Animal voices built the same way as the Voice pair: a source instrument and, where it helps, a separate body effect, all modelled from research. krill uses these engines to make whole ambient pieces. On the showcase page as "in the works", with source links instead of downloads.

Dolphin

Prototype

A cetacean voice synth. Each voice has two phonic-lip sources: a pulse source whose rate runs from rhythm (codas, click trains) to pitch (creaks, squawks, burst pulses), and a whistle of a few pure partials with no mouth formants. Biphonation couples them, so whistles carry sidebands as in orca calls.

Controls: Rate, Keytrack, Pattern, Coda, Jitter, Rubato, Ornament, Click tone and width, Ring, Vowel · whistle Pitch mode, Shape, Sweep, Time, Harmonics · Register, Biphon, Spread · Size · Mono/Poly, Glide, ADSR · four LFOs (From/To, free or synced)

State: JavaScript reference engine, engine tests, a perf benchmark and a playable browser bench with a live DSP meter. C++ port and JUCE instrument next. Factory presets are for testing and likely stay out of the plugin.

Open: vowel peak frequencies (set by ear), MPE for per-note bends

krill

Prototype · batch 10 of 11

A generator for long ambient pieces made by a pond of alien creatures. Each creature is a source (Throat or Dolphin), with or without a mouth-tube, and rolls every sound from its own ranges. Creatures take turns, answer and copy each other; slow eras drift density and register. The pond renders to a WAV, the same file every time for the same seed Decided.

Pages: creature page (ranges, Morph, signature calls, solo render), pond page (six slots, Talks/Ignores, loudness range, key and harmonise, Overlap, Chaos, eras, preview and export), visualizer (a scope per creature: Ring, Shapes, Loop, Phase, Knot)

State: plain JavaScript that runs in the browser and in Node, with vendored, hash-checked copies of the Throat, Dolphin and mouth-tube engines. Listening round next. Not a plugin yet Open

Syrinx

Prototype · batch 03 of 11

A bird voice synth. Each voice is a syrinx of one, two or three sources, each a small nonlinear oscillator driven by air pressure and labial tension. Low notes start pulse-like, high notes near-pure; coupled sides lock, beat and throw off sidebands on their own.

Design: Duplex (songbird), Single (dove, parrot) or Triple (ovenbird, with a tracheal membrane) · play modes Handoff, Interval, Duo, Independent · Coupling and Jumps through a shared trachea · superfast-muscle Wobble · syllable Patterns with a trill limit · Size from wren to crane · Musical lock to semitones · an internal Filter so it sounds like a bird alone. 44 parameters.

State: source core works (one oscillator per voice, in tune to 0.04 cent), RK4 integration, Poly 4 voices. Sides, coupling and the bench next.

beak-tube

In design

An effect that shapes sound the way a bird's vocal tract does: a trachea, a throat cavity whose resonance follows the pitch it hears (how songbirds sing pure tones), and a beak or an inflated sac. Makes no sound of its own Decided; Syrinx's partner, as mouth-tube is Throat's.

Design: modes Song, Parrot (tongue formants), Sac (dove coo), Boom (cassowary) · Trachea length and loss, Larynx, Track, Tune, Lag, Ring, Gape and Gape follow, Tongue, Sac tune, Inflate, Body · Size matched to Syrinx · stereo with a tracker per channel. 21 parameters, no LFOs.

State: design, build plan and research notes in the repo; reference engine first.

More ideas from the same research thread (tymbal, swim bladder, croak, stridulation, parasaurolophus, multiple mouths, geophony and others) are kept in synth-ideas.md and aren't on any roadmap yet.

The Voice pair

A source–filter voice split across two products, each useful alone: Throat makes the sound, mouth-tube shapes it. Both are built and on the showcase page with download links.

Throat

v0.1.0 built

A playable synth that models a larynx. The only sound source in the pair Decided, including voiceless breath and whisper.

Engine: each fold cycle is an LF (Liljencrants–Fant) glottal pulse, with breath noise that pulses with every opening, per-cycle irregularity and subharmonics. Blends freely between modal, breathy, pressed, creaky, falsetto-like, growled and voiceless.

Controls: Voicing, Breathy, Tense, Creaky, Thin, Growl · Jitter, Shimmer · Transpose, Detune, Glide, Mono/Poly (8 voices) · ADSR · Source (flow or derivative), Volume · user presets

Formats: AU, VST3 instrument, Standalone · macOS universal · 32 samples latency · plugin code Thrt

Later: a two-mass physical mode as an experiment.

mouth-tube

v0.1.0 built

An audio effect that shapes sound the way a vocal tract does. Makes no sound of its own Decided; works on drums, synths and field recordings as well as Throat.

Engine: a Kelly-Lochbaum waveguide of 40 sections from glottis to lips, plus a nasal branch at the velum. The controls describe a mouth and the tube shape follows; vowels, nasals and closures come from the shape alone, with no presets inside the engine. The tube runs at its own rate, so length scales formants smoothly. The planned formant-filter stage was skipped.

Controls: tongue position pad (vowel chart), Tip, Jaw, Lips opening and Round, Nasal, Length 7–35 cm, Damping · Spread, Radiation, Mix, Output · user presets

Formats: AU, VST3 effect, Standalone · macOS universal · dual-mono stereo · 28 samples latency at 48 kHz · plugin code Mtub

Fricatives: optionally take Throat's separate noise stream by sidechain and inject it at the constriction, for sharp s/sh/f Open

05

Technical architecture

Architecture

Every plugin follows the Bandwidth Crusher layout. The DSP has no framework dependency, so it can be tested alone and moved to another framework if licensing ever demands it.

LayerStandard
DSP coreSingle header, e.g. source/CrusherDSP.h. Everything allocated in prepare(); process() never allocates, locks or does I/O. Fixed 10 ms control blocks so the sound is buffer-size independent; latency reported to the host.
Reference + testsThe reference engine is the source of truth: Python in the Bandwidth repos (reference/*.py, tests/compare.py), JavaScript in Throat, mouth-tube, Dolphin and Syrinx (reference/*.js, tests/compare.js, run with Node). krill vendors those engines unmodified and checks their hashes. A C++ CLI build is compared against it (transparency, buffer-size independence, statistics). Throat and mouth-tube also have a plugin_test that checks the JUCE wrapper.
WrapperJUCE 8 via CMake. Formats AU, VST3, Standalone. Parameter ranges must match the UI's table exactly; the host talks in normalised 0–1 values.
UI bridgeWeb view serves the HTML from binary data. C++ → JS: param events and ~30 fps view frames. JS → C++: setParam, gesture begin/end, uiReady. Outside the plugin the page runs a small simulation.
IdentityCompany "Bytebent Audio", manufacturer code Bytb, bundle ID com.bytebent.<name>, a unique 4-letter plugin code (Crusher Bwcr, Brawl Bwbr, Throat Thrt, mouth-tube Mtub). Version comes from CMake and shows in the header as "v0.1.1 · Bytebent Audio".
Presetssource/PresetManager.h (Throat, mouth-tube): user presets in ~/Music/Bytebent/<Plugin>/Presets, browsed with ◀ name ▶ and Save; a * marks unsaved changes. No factory presets yet.
CILinux jobs run the DSP tests; macOS 14 job builds a universal binary, ad-hoc signs, runs auval and pluginval at strictness 7, zips with Install.command + INSTALL.txt, publishes as the latest release.
InstallCopies to the user plug-in folders and clears quarantine. Installs alongside, never over, the old fl-vibez copy, so existing Ableton sets keep working.
Git hygieneCommits authored as bytebent <bytebent@users.noreply.github.com>. No personal names, emails, tokens or paths in history. Exception still to fix: Brawl's only commit is authored as "Claude".
Planning filesNewer repos (krill, Syrinx, beak-tube) start with design.md (every parameter, tagged Decided / Proposed / Open), plan.md (numbered batches, each ending in passing tests and a commit, with a "You" check before the next), research.md (papers and the numbers taken from them) and tests.md (results per batch, with pictures).
PerformanceEngines ship a tools/perf.js that reports the share of one CPU core per scenario at 48 kHz, worst case included. Work that isn't audible is skipped (idle voices, inactive LFOs, silent creatures).
CI costPublic repos on standard GitHub-hosted runners (including macos-14) use no Actions minutes, so Mac builds on every push are fine for public plugin repos. A private repo would use minutes, and macOS minutes cost the most.
06

How a plugin gets made

Pipeline
1ConceptOne-line idea, controls list, what the live view shows.
2UI mockupStatic HTML in the kit. Judge the look before any DSP.
3Reference + benchEngine in Python or JavaScript, wrapped in a browser page: load audio or play live, listen, download.
4C++ + JUCEPort, compare tests, wire the mockup to real parameters.
5CI + releaseMac build, validation, packaged download, real-DAW test.

Brawl showed why step 3 matters: testing revealed that measuring each side's claim as "distance from my input" turned a silent sidechain into a gate, so the rule was changed to "claim only where your own input has changed". Design changes are cheap in the reference and expensive in C++.

Release checklist Proposed Multiple DAWs, sample rates and buffer sizes; automation; save and reload projects; presets; a short before/after demo video; a manual; a support contact.

08

Business model

Business.xls
Everything in this section is Proposed from our chats. None of it is committed, and the licence points should be checked against JUCE's full terms. I'm not a lawyer or financial adviser.

Licensing

  • Crusher is currently open source under the AGPLv3 (required by JUCE's free licence when the code is public).
  • JUCE 8 tiers: Starter free up to $20k/year revenue with no splash screen; Indie $40/month or $800 perpetual up to $300k/year; Pro unlimited. Confirm Starter allows closed source before relying on it.
  • Staying on JUCE is the recommendation. If ever needed, iPlug2 is the nearest permissive alternative, and the framework-free DSP keeps that switch small.

Platforms and signing

  • Mac builds are ad-hoc signed only. Paid Mac plugins should be notarised (Apple Developer, ~$99/year). Until then: Mac as pay-what-you-want with a suggested price.
  • Windows builds don't exist yet and most buyers are on Windows. Needs a CI job and a Windows tester (Waveform Free handles sidechains).
