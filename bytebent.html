Bandwidth Crusher
Out now · v0.1.1

Bandwidth Crusher

Your audio, but only N kB can change per second.

A short loop plays on repeat while the input tries to rewrite it, under a strict byte budget. Whatever doesn't get updated keeps playing old audio: smears, ghosts, stutters and fizz.

  • Budget from 0.5 to 1000 kB/s, the one knob that matters
  • Greedy or random updates: clean repair vs. fizzy chaos
  • Loops from 1 ms buzz to 4 s washes, tempo-synced if you like
  • Live loop view shows fresh and stale audio as it plays

Bandwidth Brawl
New · v0.1.0

Bandwidth Brawl

Two tracks. One loop. One budget. Let them fight.

Your track and a sidechain share a single loop and a single byte budget. Each side only claims budget where its own input has changed; the loser freezes on stale audio until it changes enough to win back its ground.

  • The two parts take turns, and the rhythm comes from the music
  • Put it on a pad with drums as the rival: ducking that stalls instead of dips
  • Territory view shows who owns which part of the loop
  • Bias, Sharpness and Hold shape the fight from gentle to brutal

Throat

Throat

A larynx you can play.

A synth that models vocal folds. Every cycle is a real glottal pulse, with breath that puffs on each opening, uneven timing and subharmonics. Blend from breathy to pressed, croaky fry to thin falsetto, growl or pure whisper.

  • Six phonation knobs that mix freely: Voicing, Breathy, Tense, Creaky, Thin, Growl
  • Jitter and shimmer for the small unsteadiness of a real voice
  • Mono with glide, or 8-voice poly for choirs of throats
  • Save your own presets; made to feed mouth-tube, but raw it's a strange, buzzy lead

mouth-tube
New · v0.1.0

mouth-tube

Any sound, through a mouth.

An effect that sends your audio down a 40-section tube from throat to lips, with a nose that opens at the back. Move the tongue, jaw and lips and the vowels come out of the shape itself. It makes no sound of its own.

  • Drag the tongue around a vowel chart; tip, jaw and lips do the rest
  • Open the velum for nasals; close the lips and it hums through the nose
  • Tract length from 7 to 35 cm: a tiny mouth or a giant one
  • Put it on Throat to make it talk, or on drums, synths and field recordings

Dolphin
Prototype · browser bench

Dolphin

Clicks, codas and whistles from two phonic lips.

A cetacean voice synth. Each voice has two sources, as toothed whales do: a pulse source whose rate runs from rhythm (codas, click trains) up into pitch (creaks, squawks, burst pulses), and a whistle of a few pure partials. Biphonation couples them, so whistles carry sidebands like orca calls.

  • One Rate control carries clicks into buzz, burst pulse and tone
  • Coda patterns with jitter and rubato, free or synced to tempo
  • Drawn whistle shapes and sweeps for each note
  • Size scales the animal, and everything stays inside human hearing

krill
Prototype · listening round

krill

A pond of alien creatures that talk to each other.

A generator for long ambient pieces. Each creature is built from bytebent engines (Throat, Dolphin and mouth-tube), rolls every sound from its own ranges, and takes turns, answers and copies the others. The pond renders to a WAV file of any length.

  • Design creatures by ranges: narrow is predictable, wide is strange
  • Up to six creatures per pond, each in its own lane and colour
  • Same pond and seed give the same piece, bit for bit
  • A visualizer draws each creature as a scope of its own voice

Syrinx
Prototype · engine in progress

Syrinx

A bird's voice box, both sides at once.

A bird voice synth. Each voice is a syrinx with one, two or three sources, each a small oscillator driven by air pressure and tension, the way birds make sound. Low notes start rich and pulse-like, high notes start almost pure, and coupled sources lock, beat and pulse on their own.

  • Duplex, Single or Triple sources: songbird, dove or ovenbird
  • Handoff sends low notes to the left side and high ones to the right, mid-sweep
  • Patterns turn a held key into syllables and trills
  • Size goes from wren to crane; Musical lock snaps it to semitones

beak-tube
In design

beak-tube

Any sound, through a beak.

An effect that sends audio up a trachea, through a throat cavity that retunes itself to the pitch it hears, and out of a beak or an inflated sac. Songbirds use that trick to turn a buzzy source into a pure tone. It makes no sound of its own.

  • The throat resonance follows the pitch, the way songbirds sing pure notes
  • Song, Parrot, Sac and Boom modes: finch, parakeet, dove coo, cassowary
  • Size matches Syrinx, so the same value gives the same bird
  • Put it on Syrinx, or on voices, synths and drums

About

ReadMe.txt

What these are

Small, odd effects and instruments for producers who like happy accidents. Some take an idea from computing, like data compression or error-correcting codes. Others model how bodies actually make sound, from the human larynx to dolphins and songbirds, then push past what any real animal can do.

Many of them take a second input through your DAW's sidechain, so one track can reshape another.

Compatibility

Bandwidth Crusher, Bandwidth Brawl, Throat and mouth-tube run as AU and VST3 on macOS, Apple Silicon and Intel, plus a standalone app. Dolphin, Syrinx and beak-tube are on their way to the same formats. krill runs in the browser and renders WAV files.

All the code is public on GitHub.
